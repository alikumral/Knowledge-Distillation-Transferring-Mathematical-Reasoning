## Abstract

Large language models for multi-step reasoning are expensive to serve, motivating the transfer of reasoning ability from large *teacher* models into small *student* models. We study sequence-level chain-of-thought (CoT) distillation from **Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct** into **Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct** on grade-school math, using a tight, fully reproducible recipe that runs on free/low-cost Google Colab. We generate teacher CoT traces over the GSM8K training set, filter them by rejection sampling against gold answers (81.5% acceptance), and fine-tune the student with QLoRA under five carefully controlled conditions. Distillation works and is statistically overwhelming: the distilled 1B student improves from **38.1%** to **46.9%** on GSM8K test (+8.9 pp, McNemar *p* < 1e-9), and crucially **outperforms a student trained on GSM8K's own gold solutions** by +13 pp (*p* < 1e-17), showing that teacher-generated reasoning carries signal beyond the labeled data. We also report two robust **negative** findings: (1) fine-tuning on gold CoT *degrades* accuracy below the zero-shot baseline, and (2) using three teacher traces per problem is *significantly worse* than one (a ~6σ effect across three seeds), contrary to our hypothesis. Gains do not transfer out of distribution: accuracy on a held-out MATH subset drops relative to zero-shot. Finally, we find the practical efficiency advantage of the 1B student is **memory** (5.2× lower peak VRAM), not latency, because the distilled student inherits the teacher's verbosity. We release code, the cleaned trace dataset, and the trained adapter.

## 1. Introduction

Deploying LLMs for reasoning tasks—math, code, structured planning—is bottlenecked by the cost and latency of large models. A 1B model is roughly 8× cheaper to serve than an 8B model and dramatically cheaper than a 70B model; if a small student can be brought close to a larger teacher's quality on a target task, edge inference, on-device assistants, and high-throughput batch jobs all become viable. Chain-of-thought distillation—training a small model on the step-by-step reasoning of a larger one—has been shown to yield large gains, but published recipes are often computationally heavy or rely on closed teacher APIs.

We ask a focused, practical question: **how far can a 1B student be pushed on grade-school math reasoning by distilling from a *small, open* 8B teacher, under a single-digit-GPU-hour budget, with rigorous baselines?** Rather than chasing a single headline number, we build five tightly controlled conditions so that every comparison isolates one variable, and we report honest negative results where they occur.

**Contributions.**
1. **A tight, fully open, reproducible CoT-distillation recipe** (8B teacher, not 70B; QLoRA; runs end-to-end on Colab) with resumable pipeline stages and released artifacts.
2. **Rigorous evidence that distillation works and that the teacher signal exceeds gold labels** (H1, H2), confirmed with bootstrap confidence intervals and exact McNemar tests across three random seeds.
3. **Two robust negative findings:** training on gold CoT *hurts* relative to zero-shot, and *more* teacher traces per problem (3 vs 1) is significantly *worse*—a ~6σ effect.
4. **An efficiency analysis showing the real lever is memory, not latency**, due to verbosity inheritance, plus a per-difficulty and qualitative error analysis and an out-of-distribution (MATH) evaluation showing no transfer.

## 2. Related Work

**Knowledge distillation.** Hinton et al. [1] introduced distillation via soft-label matching. For autoregressive LLMs, storing teacher logits is impractical, so *sequence-level* distillation—training the student on text generated by the teacher—has become standard. MiniLLM [2] and DistiLLM [3] refine the divergence objective and data construction, and report sequence-level distillation as competitive. The DeepSeek-R1 report [4] demonstrates that fine-tuning small models on long CoT traces from a strong teacher transfers reasoning effectively.

**CoT distillation for reasoning.** Magister et al. [5] ("Teaching Small Language Models to Reason") show that fine-tuning small models on teacher CoT improves multi-step arithmetic, and that *rejection sampling* against gold answers improves trace quality—the recipe we follow.

**Efficient fine-tuning.** LoRA [6] freezes the base model and learns low-rank adapter matrices; QLoRA [7] adds 4-bit NF4 quantization of the frozen base, enabling fine-tuning of billion-parameter models on a single consumer GPU. We use QLoRA throughout.

**Benchmarks.** GSM8K [8] is the canonical grade-school math word-problem benchmark with gold CoT; MATH [9] contains harder competition-level problems and serves as our out-of-distribution test.

Our work differs from prior distillation papers in deliberately staying small and open (8B teacher, 1B student, single-GPU budget) and in emphasizing controlled baselines and honest negative results over a single benchmark number.

## 3. Method

### 3.1 Overview

Our pipeline has four stages: (1) teacher CoT generation, (2) rejection sampling and dataset construction, (3) QLoRA fine-tuning of the student, and (4) evaluation. All logic lives in a small installable package (`mathdistill`); each stage reads and writes versioned JSONL artifacts so that long runs on Colab are resumable across disconnects.

### 3.2 Comparison conditions

We train and evaluate five conditions, designed so that each pairwise comparison isolates a single factor:

| # | Condition | Trained? | Training data | Isolates |
|---|-----------|----------|---------------|----------|
| i | Zero-shot 1B | no | — | lower bound |
| ii | 1B + gold CoT | yes | GSM8K gold solutions | value of fine-tuning on labels alone |
| iii | 1B + 1 teacher CoT | yes | 1 accepted teacher trace / problem | distillation effect (H1) |
| iv | 1B + 3 teacher CoT | yes | up to 3 accepted traces / problem | effect of more traces (H3) |
| v | 8B teacher | no | — | upper bound |

Conditions ii–iv are trained with **three random seeds**; conditions i and v are deterministic (greedy decoding) and run once. Crucially, the gold and teacher training targets are formatted identically (same system prompt, same final-answer line) so that the **only** difference between conditions ii and iii is the *content* of the reasoning, making the H2 comparison fair.

### 3.3 Teacher CoT generation

We run Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct in 4-bit NF4 (double quantization, bf16 compute) over all 7,473 GSM8K training problems. To obtain diverse traces while controlling VRAM, we sample in **three independent passes** (one trace per problem per pass, temperature 0.7, top-*p* 0.9, max 256 new tokens), rather than drawing *k* samples in a single decoding call. Each pass uses a different random seed and appends to a shared trace pool; generation is resumable at the (problem, sample) level. This produced 22,449 raw traces.

### 3.4 Rejection sampling and dataset construction

We parse the final numerical answer from each trace and keep only traces matching the gold label. **18,287 / 22,449 traces were accepted (81.5%)**, covering 6,922 of 7,473 problems with ≥1 correct trace and 5,081 problems with ≥3. From the accepted pool we build three supervised fine-tuning (SFT) datasets: **gold** (7,473 examples), **teacher_1** (6,922; one trace/problem), and **teacher_3** (18,263; up to three/problem).

### 3.5 QLoRA fine-tuning

The student (Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct) is loaded in 4-bit NF4; we attach LoRA adapters (rank 16, α=32, dropout 0.05) on all attention and MLP projections. We train for 3 epochs with learning rate 2e-4 (cosine schedule, 3% warmup), effective batch size 16 (per-device 8 × grad-accum 2), bf16, gradient checkpointing, and the paged 8-bit AdamW optimizer, with a maximum sequence length of 1,024. Training one condition takes roughly 18–49 minutes on an A100.

*Honest implementation note (loss masking).* We intended **completion-only** loss masking (loss computed only on the assistant's reasoning, not the prompt). The TRL version available on Colab had removed the collator we targeted and gated its built-in masking behind chat-template markers absent from Llama-3, so all conditions were trained with **full-sequence loss** instead. Because this choice is applied identically to every condition, it does not affect the *relative* comparisons that our hypotheses test; we treat completion-only masking as future work (Section 7).

### 3.6 Evaluation protocol

We evaluate with greedy decoding (deterministic) and extract the final numerical answer using a fixed marker plus fallbacks (`\boxed{}`, `####`, last number), with numeric normalization. The primary metric is **GSM8K test exact-match accuracy** (1,319 problems). Secondary metrics are MATH out-of-distribution accuracy (a frozen, level-stratified subset of 167 problems), single-example inference latency (batch size 1), peak inference VRAM, and generation length. We quantify uncertainty with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals over test items and, for paired comparisons on the same items, **exact McNemar tests**; for trained conditions we additionally report mean ± standard deviation over three seeds.

## 4. Experimental Setup

- **Models.** Teacher: `meta-llama/Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct`. Student: `meta-llama/Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct`. Both 4-bit NF4, double quant, bf16 compute.
- **Data.** `openai/gsm8k` (config `main`): 7,473 train / 1,319 test. Out-of-distribution: a frozen stratified subset of `qwedsacf/competition_math` (MATH), 167 problems ("MATH-167").
- **Hardware.** Google Colab; teacher generation and most evaluation on A100, training on A100. *Note:* our proposal specified latency on a T4; we report latency on the A100 actually used and treat the absolute latency numbers as hardware-specific (the cross-model *ratios* are the meaningful quantity).
- **Reproducibility.** All hyperparameters are in version-controlled YAML configs; every run records its seed and configuration. Code, the cleaned teacher-trace dataset, and the best adapter are released.

## 5. Results

### 5.1 Main results

Table 1 reports accuracy on GSM8K and MATH, with 95% bootstrap CIs (seed 0) and seed mean ± std for the trained conditions.

**Table 1 — Main results.**

| Condition | GSM8K acc. | GSM8K 95% CI | MATH acc. | MATH 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (i) Zero-shot 1B | 0.3806 | [0.355, 0.406] | 0.3353 | [0.264, 0.407] |
| (ii) Gold CoT | 0.3374 | [0.312, 0.363] | 0.2096 | [0.150, 0.275] |
| (iii) **teacher_1** | **0.4693 ± 0.0011** | [0.441, 0.495] | 0.2914 ± 0.0198 | [0.204, 0.335] |
| (iv) teacher_3 | 0.4433 ± 0.0077 | [0.407, 0.461] | 0.2615 ± 0.0056 | [0.204, 0.335] |
| (v) 8B teacher | 0.8287 | [0.808, 0.849] | 0.5389 | [0.461, 0.617] |

**Statistical significance (exact McNemar, paired on the 1,319 GSM8K items).**

| Comparison | discordant (A-only / B-only) | *p* | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1: teacher_1 vs zero-shot | 236 / 121 | 1.16e-09 | distillation works *** |
| H2: teacher_1 vs gold | 290 / 118 | 8.22e-18 | teacher ≫ gold *** |
| H3: teacher_1 vs teacher_3 | 199 / 154 | 1.91e-02 | one trace **beats** three * |
| gold vs zero-shot | 172 / 229 | 5.10e-03 | gold **hurts** ** |

**Findings.**
- **H1 (distillation works): confirmed, overwhelmingly.** teacher_1 improves over zero-shot by +8.9 pp (*p* < 1e-9). The seed std of 0.0011 shows the result is extremely stable.
- **H2 (teacher > gold): confirmed, overwhelmingly.** teacher_1 beats gold by +13.2 pp (*p* < 1e-17). Teacher-generated reasoning provides signal beyond what the labeled data contains.
- **H3 (more traces help): reversed and robust.** teacher_1 (0.4693 ± 0.0011) significantly *beats* teacher_3 (0.4433 ± 0.0077); the +2.6 pp gap is ≈ 5.9× the pooled seed std and significant at *p* = 0.019. More teacher traces made the student *worse*.
- **Gold fine-tuning hurts.** Condition ii falls 4.3 pp *below* zero-shot (*p* = 0.005).

### 5.2 Accuracy by difficulty

We bucket the 1,319 test problems into easy/medium/hard tertiles by gold-CoT length. [**Figure F1** — `results/figures/F1_difficulty_accuracy.png`.]

**Table 2 — Accuracy by difficulty (seed 0).**

| Condition | Easy | Medium | Hard |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zero-shot 1B | 0.509 | 0.397 | 0.232 |
| Gold | 0.502 | 0.327 | 0.177 |
| teacher_1 | 0.626 | 0.446 | 0.326 |
| teacher_3 | 0.584 | 0.418 | 0.294 |
| 8B teacher | 0.889 | 0.868 | 0.727 |

Distillation helps across all difficulty levels, with large absolute gains on both easy (+11.7 pp) and hard (+9.4 pp) problems. The largest remaining student–teacher gap is on hard problems (0.326 vs 0.727), confirming the 1B student still cannot match multi-step reasoning that tracks several quantities at once. Gold training notably *degrades* medium and hard problems (−7.0 and −5.5 pp), consistent with the verbosity analysis below.

### 5.3 Generation length and the verbosity tax

[**Figure F2** — `results/figures/F2_generation_lengths.png`.] Median generated tokens: **gold 68, zero-shot 156, teacher_1 172, teacher_3 174, 8B teacher 181.** Two observations: (1) **gold training collapses reasoning length** to 68 tokens—the model learned to answer tersely rather than reason, explaining why it underperforms even zero-shot; (2) the distilled students **inherit the teacher's verbosity** (≈172 vs 181 tokens), nearly matching the 8B model's output length.

### 5.4 Efficiency

**Table 3 — Efficiency (batch size 1).**

| Condition | Latency (s/problem) | Peak inference VRAM |
|---|---|---|
| Zero-shot 1B | 6.17 | 1.09 GB |
| teacher_1 (distilled) | 12.39 | 1.13 GB |
| teacher_3 | 12.91 | 1.13 GB |
| 8B teacher | 13.73 | 5.87 GB |

The headline efficiency result is counter-intuitive: at batch size 1, the distilled 1B student (12.4 s) is **barely faster than the 8B teacher** (13.7 s), because verbosity inheritance roughly doubles its output length relative to zero-shot (6.2 s). The genuine advantage is **memory: the student uses 5.2× less peak VRAM** (1.13 vs 5.87 GB), which translates into far higher achievable concurrency/throughput on fixed hardware. In other words, distillation buys *density*, not single-query speed.

### 5.5 Out-of-distribution (MATH)

Distillation does **not** transfer to MATH. teacher_1 scores 0.2914 ± 0.0198, *below* the zero-shot 0.3353, and far below the teacher's 0.5389. Notably teacher_1 and teacher_3 are statistically indistinguishable on MATH, suggesting MATH performance is driven by base-model pretraining rather than our GSM8K fine-tuning. A manual audit of 15 MATH cases marked incorrect found only 1 false negative (LaTeX fraction formatting, `-\frac12` vs `-\frac{1}{2}`); we therefore treat MATH accuracies as slight lower bounds, but the absence of OOD transfer is robust.

## 6. Discussion

**Why does gold fine-tuning hurt?** GSM8K's gold solutions are terse and stylistically different from the student's natural output. Training on them with full-sequence loss pushed the student toward short, conclusory answers (median 68 tokens), suppressing the step-by-step reasoning it actually needs. The teacher's traces, by contrast, are written in the same Llama chat style the student already favors, so they reinforce rather than fight the student's prior.

**Why does one teacher trace beat three?** We offer two non-exclusive explanations. First, with three diverse traces per problem and full-sequence loss, the student fits three different surface reasoning styles for the same answer, which may blur its decoding distribution. Second, teacher_3 reaches a much lower training loss (≈0.22 vs ≈0.38) without improving test accuracy—a classic over-fitting signature. The verbosity statistics rule out a length explanation (teacher_1 and teacher_3 are essentially identical in output length), pointing to training dynamics rather than data style.

**Qualitative trace study.** Side-by-side cases illustrate the mechanism behind H1. On a multi-step "work-backwards" problem, the zero-shot model applied operations in the wrong order, while teacher_1 introduced a variable and solved the equation systematically—evidence that the student learned a *reasoning strategy*, not just answers. A representative failure shows the limit: on an overtime-pay problem teacher_1 set the problem up correctly but multiplied the overtime rate by all 45 hours instead of the 5 overtime hours—a single arithmetic slip inside otherwise correct reasoning. On easy problems both models already succeed and differ only in phrasing. (Full transcripts in Appendix B.)

**Gap closed.** The distilled student closes roughly 20% of the zero-shot-to-teacher accuracy gap (8.9 of 44.8 pp) at one-fifth the memory footprint—a meaningful but honest result at this scale.

## 7. Limitations

- **Full-sequence loss.** Due to a TRL API change we used full-sequence rather than completion-only loss. This is consistent across conditions (so comparisons are fair) but may have amplified the gold-CoT degradation; completion-only masking is the most important follow-up.
- **Single seed for some conditions.** Conditions i, ii, and v are single runs (i and v are deterministic; gold is single-seed). The gold-vs-zero-shot effect is large and significant, but multi-seed gold would tighten it.
- **Approximate MATH grading.** Our matcher is string/numeric, not a CAS; reported MATH numbers are conservative lower bounds (≈7% false-negative rate in our audit).
- **Latency hardware.** Latency was measured on A100, not the T4 in our proposal; absolute numbers are hardware-specific.
- **Possible GSM8K contamination.** The 1B model may have seen GSM8K during pretraining; we mitigate by treating MATH as the genuine-generalization signal, where we (honestly) see no gain.
- **No LoRA-rank ablation.** Time constraints precluded the planned rank-8 vs rank-16 comparison.

---

## 8. Conclusion and Future Work

Sequence-level CoT distillation from a small open 8B teacher meaningfully improves a 1B student on GSM8K (38.1% → 46.9%), and teacher-generated reasoning clearly beats the dataset's own gold solutions—both results overwhelmingly significant. Equally informative are our negative findings: gold-CoT fine-tuning *hurts*, more teacher traces *hurt*, and the gains do not transfer to MATH. The practical payoff of the small student is memory (5.2× lower VRAM), not latency, because the student inherits the teacher's verbosity.

**Future work.** (1) Completion-only loss masking (the highest-priority fix). (2) Length-controlled or quality-filtered trace selection to address the verbosity tax and the counter-productive third trace. (3) A stronger math teacher (e.g., Qwen2.5-Math-7B) and a CAS-based MATH grader. (4) Mixing in a small amount of OOD data to test whether transfer can be induced.
